Project Documentation

Project management plan (PGC) of the platform.

Project title

Credit Risk and Loan Viability Prediction Platform: a machine learning web system for lending decision support.

Problem statement and justification

Credit risk is one of the main sources of vulnerability of banks. The Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (2017) requires capital in proportion to the credit exposure, because wrong lending decisions produce non-performing loans and provisioning costs. Traditional scorecards and manual reviews capture non-linear relations among the attributes of a borrower less well than machine learning classifiers (Lessmann et al., 2015; Dastile et al., 2020).

Approving a loan that will not be repaid increases the default rate; rejecting one that would have been repaid excludes a customer and loses revenue. Manual evaluation is also slow and differs between analysts. A system is needed that estimates the risk quickly and consistently, explains its limits, and leaves the final decision with a person who gives a reason (Bussmann et al., 2021).

General objective

Develop a web platform that estimates the viability of a loan from the financial information of an applicant, using machine learning, to support the credit decision of an analyst.

Specific objectives

  1. Document the requirements and the business process of a credit request.
  2. Build a clean, validated dataset with exploratory analysis and feature engineering.
  3. Train and compare logistic regression, random forest and XGBoost, and select a champion with documented criteria.
  4. Implement, test and deploy the web system with roles, request flow, inference log and monitoring.

Reference framework

Lessmann et al. (2015) compared 41 classifiers and found that ensembles outperform individual classifiers and logistic regression. Dastile et al. (2020) reviewed credit scoring models and noted the lack of interpretability of the most complex ones. Gunnarsson et al. (2021) showed that deep networks bring little gain over tuned gradient boosting on tabular credit data. Chen and Guestrin (2016) introduced XGBoost. Bussmann et al. (2021) showed that explainable techniques make credit models understandable for risk managers.

The project therefore compares a simple baseline with two ensembles, publishes the results of each one in read-only pages, and keeps a human analyst in the decision.
