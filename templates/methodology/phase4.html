Phase 4 · {{ title }}

How good the models are, whether they overfit, whom they treat differently and what could not be validated with an expert.

Metrics and why they were chosen

  • ROC AUC is the main metric: it measures how well the model ranks defaulters above repayers for every possible threshold, and it does not depend on the class balance.
  • Recall of the default class breaks ties and is a success criterion: it is the share of defaulters that the model finds, and missing a defaulter is the costly error.
  • Precision and F1 are reported to show the price of that recall: with few defaulters, many flagged applications are false alarms.
  • Accuracy is reported but not used to decide: a model that predicts that nobody defaults would have a high accuracy on imbalanced data.
  • Validation uses stratified 5-fold cross-validation on the train split; the test split gives the final independent performance.

Team decision about the age

The team keeps Age in the official model: without it the cross-validated ROC AUC falls from {{ '%.3f'|format(w.with_age.cv.roc_auc[0]) }} to {{ '%.3f'|format(w.without_age.cv.roc_auc[0]) }}, a loss of discrimination that would make the model less useful. The audit by age range (subgroups.json) and the experiment above show the price of this choice: the champion flags {{ pct(w.by_age_range['18-24'].flagged_rate_with_age) }} of the applicants aged 18 to 24, while the real default rate of that group is {{ pct(w.by_age_range['18-24'].default_rate) }}.

Mitigation. Every rejection of an applicant aged 18 to 24 must be reviewed by a person; the model is a support tool, not an automatic decision.

Future work. Thresholds per age group. This decision was taken by the team and has not been validated by a domain expert.
